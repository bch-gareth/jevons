What this is

The Jevons is a proposed unit of account for contracts: a unit any two parties to a contract can choose to denominate it in, when the units otherwise available suit them poorly. It honours the economist W. S. Jevons, who in 1875 counted money's role as a standard of deferred payment.

Its value is derived from data integral to the Bitcoin Cash (BCH) blockchain - each block's difficulty and its coinbase - by the formula below, published in full so that anyone can check it. An oracle carries outside data onto a chain; this is a reverse oracle: it carries the chain's own data out, to the world beyond it.

The why, and the thinking behind it, are in the project's README and in the booklet Cash Unbound.

The v1 formula, in full

For each Bitcoin Cash block from height 308672 - the first block at or after 1 July 2014, 00:00 UTC - in the order of its height:

  1. The block's work, in expected hashes, from the difficulty D that the header's nBits field stands for: W = D × 2^48 / 65535   (the same number as 2^256 / target)
  2. The block's time, the header's timestamp made strictly increasing; the first block's t is its own timestamp: t = max(timestamp, previous t + 1)
  3. Mining efficiency at that time, in gigahashes per joule, a logistic curve whose midpoint, 2029104000, is 20 April 2034, 00:00 UTC: eta(t) = 700 / (1 + exp(-8e-9 × (t - 2029104000)))
  4. The block's energy-equivalent, in kWh: E = W / (eta(t) × 10^9) / 3600000
  5. The block's coinbase, in BCH: the outputs of its coinbase transaction, summed - the subsidy plus the fees: C = sum of the coinbase transaction's outputs
  6. The block's raw value, in BCH per kWh-equivalent; and 1 XJU = 1 kWh-equivalent: P = C / E    raw value in BCH per XJU = P × 1
  7. The Jevons' value is the exponential moving average of the raw value over 201600 blocks - 200 weeks of 10-minute blocks - begun at the first block's P: A = previous A + alpha × (P - previous A)    alpha = 2 / (201600 + 1)

The arithmetic is IEEE 754 double precision throughout; W is one division of two whole numbers, and C stays in whole satoshis until its one division.

v1 is frozen, public and versioned: a later version never alters v1's published series.

Check it yourself

  • v1.json - the figures above and every constant, for programs to read.
  • The per-block inputs - each block's height, timestamp, nBits and coinbase total, kept in the open and added to every hour.
  • The code - one Python file, standard library only. It fetches the chain data from a public Fulcrum server and checks what it is given: every header must link to the one before it and meet its own proof-of-work target, and every coinbase must hash to its transaction id and declare its block's height.
